## PROJECT OVERVIEW

This project combines three disciplines into a single, portfolio-defining body of work:

1. **Research Project** — rigorous investigation of the causes, patterns, and risk factors behind male suicide in the UK
2. **Data Analytics Project** — evidence-based analysis, visualisation, and predictive modelling using ONS, NHS, and Samaritans data
3. **Documentary Storytelling Project** — a scripted and interview-based short documentary bringing the human reality behind the numbers to life

Few entry-level data analytics portfolios tackle a social issue of this scale. This project demonstrates technical skills, communication skills, and real-world social impact in a single piece of work.

**Core Question:**
*Why are men in the United Kingdom significantly more likely to die by suicide, yet significantly less likely to seek help?*

## STORY STRUCTURE

### ACT 1 — THE SILENCE

**Opening sequence:**
Slow, unhurried shots of ordinary British life. A mechanic wiping his hands after a long shift at a garage. A bus driver pulling into a depot after midnight. A father sitting quietly at the dinner table, three empty seats around him. A young professional staring at a laptop in a dimly lit flat. A construction worker eating alone on a steel beam during lunch break.

**Opening Voice-Over:**
*"Every day, men go to work, provide for their families, smile at colleagues, and carry burdens nobody sees. They fix engines, drive routes, build cities, and raise children. And some of them, every single day, are holding on by a thread. Most will never speak about it. Some never get the chance."*

**Establish the reality:**
*"In the United Kingdom, three out of every four people who die by suicide are men. Suicide is the single leading cause of death for men under 50. And yet this crisis — this quiet, persistent, devastating crisis — barely makes the front page."*

Fade to title card:
**THE SILENT BURDEN**

---

### ACT 2 — THE NUMBERS

**Narration:**
*"This is not a story without evidence. The data is there. The question is whether we are willing to look at it."*

**Data presented on screen (visualised):**

- UK male suicide rate: 17.6 per 100,000 (2024) — highest this century
- Men account for 75% of all suicides in the UK
- Highest risk age group: men aged 50–54 (27.5 per 100,000)
- Suicide is the leading cause of death for men under 50
- Wales 2024 rate: 25.0 per 100,000 — up from 22.0 in 2023
- North East England has the highest regional rate in England
- Men in the most deprived areas are significantly more likely to die by suicide

**Key question posed:**

*"What happens when a man feels he has nowhere to turn?"*

**On-screen text transitions between data points:**

- 1 in 8 men in the UK has a common mental health problem
- Men are less than half as likely as women to access psychological therapy
- Men make fewer GP visits — and far fewer of those visits involve mental health
- Employment loss, relationship breakdown, and financial crisis are among the strongest predictors

*"The numbers point to a system that was not built for the way men experience distress. And a culture that taught men that asking for help is a sign of failure."*

### ACT 3 — REAL VOICES

**Interview subjects (mix of scripted characters and guide for real interviews):**

**Character 1 — The Mechanic (age 38, Midlands)**
Works long hours, divorced two years ago, sees his children every other weekend. Nobody at work knows what he goes through on the Sunday nights before his children go back.

*Interview focus: Masculinity, loneliness after divorce, fatherhood, the "provider" identity*

**Character 2 — The Bus Driver (age 52, North East England)**
Twenty-three years on the same route. Made redundant six months ago when the depot restructured. Feels invisible. His wife says he hasn't been himself in months. He says he's fine.

*Interview focus: Employment identity, redundancy, male stoicism, age-related risk*

**Character 3 — The Young Professional (age 24, London)**
First in his family to go to university. High-achieving, highly anxious. Lives alone. Performs confidence in the office. Goes home to silence. Never told anyone about the darkness he felt last winter.

*Interview focus: Social isolation, performance of success, help-seeking barriers among young men*

**Character 4 — The Veteran (age 45, Scotland)**
Left the army eight years ago. No formal diagnosis, no formal support. Finds civilian life harder than he expected. Drinks more than he should. Misses the structure and the brothers.

*Interview focus: PTSD, transition, masculine identity, veteran mental health, Scotland's elevated rates*

**Character 5 — The Bereaved Mother (age 61, Wales)**
Lost her son three years ago. He was 31. She had no idea how bad things had got. She wants people to understand what the silence costs.

*Interview focus: The impact on families, warning signs, what she wishes she had known*

**Expert voices:**

- Clinical psychologist specialising in male mental health
- Samaritans spokesperson
- HR Director from a manufacturing company with a workplace mental health programme
- GP with experience of men presenting late with mental health concerns
- Community leader from a faith organisation

**Topics covered in this act:**

- Depression and how it presents differently in men (anger, withdrawal, substance use — not always sadness)
- Financial pressure and the link between debt and suicide risk
- Masculinity norms — "man up", "get on with it", "don't show weakness"
- Divorce, separation, and loss of contact with children
- Fatherhood and the emotional weight men carry silently
- Loneliness as a public health epidemic
- Substance abuse as self-medication
- The role of faith communities in men's wellbeing

### ACT 4 — SOLUTIONS

**Narration:**
*"This is not inevitable. In the places where things are changing, the difference is not complicated. It is connection. Conversation. And the radical decision to treat men as human beings who are allowed to struggle."*

**Highlight:**

- **Movember Foundation** — global men's health charity with UK programmes
- **CALM (Campaign Against Living Miserably)** — UK-specific male mental health charity
- **Samaritans** — 116 123 — available 24/7
- **Andy's Man Club** — men's peer support groups across the UK (growing rapidly)

- **Community projects** — Men's sheds, sports groups, faith-based support

**Final sequence:**
Return to the ordinary scenes from Act 1. The mechanic. The bus driver. The father. The young professional. Now — one of them picks up the phone.

**Closing voice-over:**
*"The crisis is real. The data proves it. But data does not grieve. Data does not sit with a man at his lowest point. People do. And sometimes all it takes is one person who asks the right question — and actually waits for the honest answer."*

**Final title card:**
*"Talking is not weakness. Silence has a cost."*

## DATA ANALYTICS COMPONENT

### Dashboard Sections

| Section | Metrics |
|---------|---------|
| Overview | Total UK male suicides by year, male vs female rate comparison, rate per 100,000 |
| Age Analysis | Rates by 5-year age band: 18–24, 25–34, 35–44, 45–54, 55–65 |
| Regional Analysis | England by region, + Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland comparison |
| Trend Analysis | Year-over-year changes 2000–2024, COVID-19 impact window |
| Risk Factors | Employment correlation, relationship status, deprivation index, rural vs urban |
| Method Analysis | Breakdown of methods by age group (for policy/means restriction focus) |

### Predictive Analytics Component

**Question:** *Using UK data from 2000–2024, can we identify patterns that predict elevated suicide risk among men aged 18–65 at a regional or local authority level?*

**Approach:**
- Feature engineering: deprivation score, unemployment rate, relationship breakdown rate, alcohol consumption data, GP mental health referral rates
- Target variable: male suicide rate by local authority / year
- Models to test: Linear Regression (baseline), Random Forest (feature importance), Gradient Boosting (prediction accuracy)
- Validation: Train on 2000–2018, test on 2019–2024
- Output: Risk score by region — visualised as a predictive heatmap

**Why this matters for employers:**
This moves the project from *descriptive analytics* ("here is what happened") into *predictive analytics* ("here is where risk may be highest") — the kind of thinking NHS commissioners, public health teams, and mental health charities need.

### PHASE 3 — DATA CLEANING AND ANALYSIS (Week 3)

**Goal:** Transform raw data into reliable, analysis-ready datasets.

**Tasks:**
- Load ONS Excel file into Python (Pandas) or Excel
- Identify and handle missing values
- Standardise age categories across datasets (England, Scotland, NI may differ)
- Merge deprivation data with suicide data by local authority
- Calculate year-over-year rate changes
- Build core KPIs: total male deaths, rate per 100,000, regional rankings

**Exploratory Analysis Questions:**
1. Which age group has the highest current rate? Has this changed over 20 years?
2. Which regions have consistently elevated rates?
3. What happened to male suicide rates during COVID-19 (2020–2021)?
4. Is there a statistically significant correlation between deprivation and suicide rate?
5. How does the male rate compare to the female rate over time?

**Tools:**
- Python (Pandas, Matplotlib, Seaborn) — recommended
- Excel / Google Sheets — acceptable for initial exploration
- Jupyter Notebook — document your work as you go

**Deliverables:**

### PHASE 6 — PREDICTIVE MODEL + SCRIPT WRITING (Week 6)

**Goal A (Analytics):** Build and validate the predictive risk model.

**Predictive Modelling Steps:**
1. Prepare feature dataset: deprivation score, unemployment rate, GP referral rate, alcohol consumption, housing stress index — by local authority and year (2000–2018)
2. Target variable: male suicide rate (lagged by 1–2 years)
3. Split: train on 2000–2018, test on 2019–2024
4. Run models: Linear Regression → Random Forest → Gradient Boosting
5. Evaluate: RMSE, R², feature importance
6. Output: predicted risk scores for 2024–2026 by local authority
7. Visualise as heatmap in Power BI
